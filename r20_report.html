R20 — Blend-Rate Schedule Sweep

Replacing StreamGVE's Eq.4 blender rate with source-anchor schedules, scored under three visual-prompting regimes on 22 FiVE-Bench clips.

What R20 tests

StreamGVE blends a source anchor into the target branch at every denoising step. The mix is set by a blender rate (blender_rate = 1 ⇒ pure target/edit, 0 ⇒ pure source/preserve). The paper ties it to the noise level via Eq.4: blender_rate = 1 − ti+1ρ, so the source anchor decays on a fixed convex profile (rising 0.13 → 1.00 across the 15 steps). R20 asks whether that profile is the right one: it swaps in schedules indexed directly by the denoising step and measures the preservation ↔ editability trade-off.

Every schedule is run under three visual-prompting (VP) regimes, because the schedule and the anchor mechanism control the same quantity — how hard the target is pulled toward a reference — so a schedule that helps without an anchor may hurt with one:

No VPno anchor (R1 baseline path)
VP (§4.5)the paper's cached first frame — enters the target cache as an initial latent and fades (R7)
Persistent VPR10's re-roped anchor bank — re-stamped every rollout window, so it never fades

The 5 schedules

All five are functions of the normalised step p = i/14. s is the source-anchoring weight; blender_rate = 1 − s. Eq.4 is the reference they replace.

schedules(p)role
Eq.4 (paper)s = ti+1ρ (ρ=2)StreamGVE default — the schedule R20 replaces (convex, tied to noise level)
cos_fulls = ½(1+cos πp)cosine, holds the anchor across the whole block, releases only at the end
cos_halfs = ½(1+cos π·min(1,2p))cosine, anchor fully released by the mid-point (p=½)
cos_thirds = ½(1+cos π·min(1,3p))cosine, earliest release (p=⅓)
consts ≡ 1degenerate bracket: pure source every step — the “no-edit” floor
zeros ≡ 0degenerate bracket: no blending — the anchor never pulls (≈ R10 blend_off)

The two degenerate schedules bracket the cosines: nothing should score outside [const, zero] on preservation. A caveat from the implementation — the prev-key blend accumulates source keys in place across steps, so a schedule that reaches s=0 early does not undo what earlier steps injected; expect less separation than the curves suggest.
